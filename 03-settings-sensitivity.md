## What is cm/360?

cm/360 measures the physical distance in centimeters required to move your mouse to complete a 360-degree rotation in-game. Standardizing your sensitivity scale in KovaaK's to cm/360 allows you to communicate and adjust mouse sensitivity with complete precision across all games and titles.

- Sensitivity & cm/360 Converter: https://kovaaks.com/kovaaks/cm360-converter

## eDPI, Baseline Calibration, and Variable vs. Competitive Practice

### 1. What is eDPI and the Competitive Reference Range

While `cm/360` measures universal physical displacement across the mousepad, **eDPI (Effective Dots Per Inch)** quantifies effective sensitivity within a specific game engine:

$$\text{eDPI} = \text{Mouse DPI} \times \text{In-Game Sensitivity}$$

- **Competitive Reference (Valorant):** The professional player average sits between **250 and 280 eDPI** (equivalent to `0.31–0.35` at `800 DPI`, or roughly **46 to 52 cm/360°**).
- **Physical Baseline Calibration (~60 cm/360° at 800 DPI):** A practical biomechanical baseline for Tactical FPS is calibrating your sensitivity so a comfortable sweep from the left edge to the right edge of your usable mousepad area (~30 cm) produces a **180°** turn in-game (~60 cm/360°).

### 2. Variable Practice (Randomizer) vs. Pre-Competitive Familiarity

- **In the Aim Trainer (Variable Practice / Reconsolidation):** Switching sensitivity by category (as shown in the tables above) or using a sensitivity randomizer within a controlled envelope ($\pm 10\%$ to $\pm 25\%$) drives reconsolidation of your Generalized Motor Program (Wymbs, Bastian & Celnik, 2016), sharpening coordination across fingertips, wrist, and forearm.
- **In Competitive Play (Long-Term Stability):** For tournaments and high-stakes ranked play, commit to a fixed base sensitivity in your primary FPS for at least **3 consecutive weeks** before judging its effectiveness. When fine-tuning is needed, adjust in small **5% to 10%** increments and avoid abrupt changes right before official matches.

## Mousepad Tribology and Poron Base Densities

Performance in micro-adjustments and continuous tracking depends heavily on **tribology** (the science of friction and lubrication between interacting surfaces) between your mouse skates (PTFE/glass) and the mousepad surface:

1. **Static Friction ($\mu_s$):** The force required to initiate mouse movement from a dead stop. Excessively high static friction causes *stick-slip* (the mouse "sticks" and lurches when attempting 1–2 pixel micro-adjustments in *Static/Micro* or *Smooth Tracking*).
2. **Dynamic Friction ($\mu_k$):** The resistance felt while the mouse is already gliding in continuous motion. Governs smoothness in *Tracking*.
3. **Stopping Power:** The combined ability of the surface weave and foam base to absorb kinetic energy at the end of an explosive flick.

### Cellular Urethane (Poron) Base Hardness

High-end mousepads (such as the Artisan lineup and equivalents) use **Poron** bases in three densities that directly alter biomechanics:

| Base Density | Mechanical Behavior | Primary Use Case |
|---|---|---|
| **MID (Firm)** | Dense foam that barely compresses under vertical load. Keeps the static-to-dynamic friction ratio 100% predictable and uniform. | Reactive/Precise *Tracking* and players who maintain a light hand without vertical tension. |
| **SOFT (Balanced)** | Allows slight compression under the fingertips. Delivers an unhindered glide when relaxed and progressive *stopping power* when pressing down during braking. | True *All-Rounder* across Click Timing, Target Switching, and Tracking. |
| **XSOFT (Extra Soft)** | High elastic deformation. Pressing the fingers or mouse into the pad brakes the cursor almost instantly, but severely punishes a *death grip* (involuntary vertical tension bogs down the glide). | *Static/Tac-FPS* demanding maximum stopping power, provided tension is strictly controlled. |

## Clinical Ergonomics (1HP Protocol) and Strength Training Interference

According to esports sports medicine guidelines (**1HP — Dr. Matthew Hwu**):

1. **Neutral Joint Alignment:** Adjust chair and desk height so the elbow forms an approximate **90°** angle, with the forearm stably supported and the wrist in a neutral posture (avoiding excessive dorsal extension or ulnar deviation).
2. **Phasic Tension vs. Tonic Tension (*Death Grip*):** Muscular contraction used to accelerate or brake the mouse must be **phasic** (lasting fractions of a second before immediate relaxation). Keeping finger and carpal flexors continuously contracted throughout a run (*death grip*) compresses the carpal tunnel, accelerates peripheral fatigue, amplifies motor noise, and leads to overuse tendinopathies.
3. **Acute Gym Interference (Heavy Strength Training):** High-intensity resistance training (especially pulling movements, deadlifts, pull-ups, and curls requiring maximal isometric grip) induces acute central nervous system fatigue and fine motor tremor in the upper limbs for several hours. **Practical rule:** always complete your high-precision aim training and ranked matches **before** heavy upper-body gym sessions, or allow a recovery window of at least 4 to 6 hours.
