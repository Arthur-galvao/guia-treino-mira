# Daily Routine Overview

This is the exact sequence to follow during every training session. Do not skip steps: each stage prepares your mechanics for the next.

## 1. Warm-up and Technical Foundation (Daily)

Play the **Flick Tech** playlist by thrFPS in KovaaK's. Share code: `KovaaKsSwitchingSeagreenHeaven`.

Only advance to the next task after hitting your daily **Threshold** on the current task. (What a Threshold is and how to calculate it is covered in Part 2.)

## 2. Weakness Development (Main Training)

Play the **Viscose Benchmark** by robinwobin.

Check your tracker and identify your lowest-performing scenarios. Grind those specific scenarios until they rank up, rather than running through the entire benchmark every session. The same Threshold rule applies here: never skip a task without hitting your target value for the day.

## 3. Progress Tracking

Log your scores and thresholds using the Escalate format. The full logging syntax and examples are detailed in Part 5.

## 4. In-Game Movement and Mechanics Training

Follow the Valorant movement training plan developed by KoreyVAL, utilizing The Range and Deathmatch. Detailed drills and level criteria are covered in Part 6.

## 5. Periodization and Time Management (30/70 and 60/40 Rules)

### The 30/70 Rule (Aim Trainer Allocation Cap)

Aim trainers isolate raw mouse control, but they do not replace tactical reading, map-specific crosshair placement, or native weapon recoil in your primary title.

- **Hard Cap (30/70):** Never allocate more than **30%** of your total gaming hours to external aim trainers; reserve at least **70%** for practice and competition inside your main FPS.
- **Professional Esports Time Distribution:**
  - **10% to 15%** — Aim Trainers (KovaaK's / Aim Lab): neuromuscular isolation and mechanical flaw correction.
  - **50% to 60%** — Team Scrims and Tactical Practice: coordinated execution under match pressure.
  - **15% to 20%** — Ranked Queues / PUGs: individual mechanical volume and duel maintenance.
  - **10% to 15%** — VOD Review and Theory: auditing positioning, movement economy, and decision-making.

### The 60/40 Weakness Focus Rule and Neuroplasticity Zone

During the time allocated to your Aim Trainer:
- **60% to 70% of volume** must directly target your weakest mechanical category (identified via the Viscose/Voltaic Benchmark).
- **30% to 40% of volume** maintains and sharpens your strongest categories.
- **Optimal Difficulty Zone (70% to 85% accuracy):** Scenarios that are too easy (>95% effortless accuracy) induce zero neural adaptation; scenarios that are overly punishing (<50% accuracy) reinforce parasitic tension and bad biomechanical habits.

### Structured 30-Minute Session Protocol (6 Blocks)

For time-constrained days or structured pre-competition warm-ups, divide a 30-minute session into six progressive blocks:

| Block | Minutes | Mechanical Focus | Execution Directive |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Joint Warm-up** | Min 1–3 | Smooth / Precise Tracking | Moderate speed, relaxed grip, and synovial warm-up across shoulder, wrist, and fingers. |
| **2. Static Precision** | Min 4–8 | Static Clicking | *Bardpill* method: straight lines, smooth deceleration, and foveal confirmation (>90% accuracy). |
| **3. Mixed Tracking** | Min 9–14 | Control & Reactive Tracking | Arm/wrist synergy and pure visual reading without prediction (*Edge Tracking / Underaiming*). |
| **4. Multi-Target Switching** | Min 15–20 | Speed & Evasive Switching | Keep fire button (LMB) held 100% continuously, straight paths, and immediate stopping power. |
| **5. Speed Ceiling** | Min 21–25 | Dynamic Clicking & Reflex | Controlled speed-pushing within the 70%–85% accuracy window (*Hit Confirming*). |
| **6. In-Game Transfer** | Min 26–30 | The Range / Deathmatch | Counter-strafing, head-level crosshair placement, and native recoil integration. |

### Weekly Periodization Alternative: VDIM (Voltaic Daily Improvement Method)

Created by **Lowgravity56 (LG56)**, the VDIM framework structures progression into 7-day weekly microcycles, dedicating each day of the week to a single fundamental subcategory (e.g., Monday = Static Clicking; Tuesday = Dynamic Clicking; Wednesday = Smooth Tracking; Thursday = Reactive Tracking; Friday = Speed Switching; Saturday = Evasive Switching; Sunday = Benchmark/Rest). It serves as an excellent structured alternative when you have 45 to 60 minutes daily for dedicated mechanics.
